Our story

SOV started on the other side of a finished wall. For fourteen years I have sold, delivered and been paid for murals, placements and cultural programs, and most of what made it hard happened around the work: the scope nobody wrote down, the invoice I had to chase, the client who wasn't sure who to trust.

Micah Walker, founder and CEO

The industry we work in

Some organizations buy creative work because the experience is the product. We work with three of them.

Experiential brands
Boutique hotels, restaurants, private clubs and destination spaces, where the room is part of what people buy, remember and come back to.
Nonprofits
Organizations that need to show the communities they serve in a way those communities recognize.
Cultural institutions
Museums, festivals and arts councils that hold a place's memory and need it carried forward with care.

What they need is culturally grounded talent that can deliver three things: premium creative assets like commissioned artwork, murals, installations, photography and film; branding that gives a place or a mission its visual language; and digital archives, the lasting record of every work, its files, its rights and its care.

Today most of that work is bought one of three ways. A buyer hires an independent artist or designer directly and becomes the project manager. A consultant advises, usually for a percentage the buyer never sees broken out. Or a directory lists names and leaves the rest to everyone else. In every case somebody still has to write the brief, agree on terms, collect insurance, track revisions, sign off on each stage and release payment. For a busy team, that administrative weight is the reason good projects stall.

The artists and designers carry it too. They write proposals for free, work on handshake agreements and wait on invoices. And the gaps fall hardest on the people who already get seen the least.

What started it

I was born in Brooklyn and raised in Greensboro. I founded Micah The Artyst in 2018 as a Bonner Scholar at Guilford College, and I went full time in 2023. Since then I have contributed to 22 murals across North Carolina, had my artwork distributed nationally through HomeGoods, received three artist residencies, and served as Director of Visual Arts and Innovation for the Raleigh Film and Arts Festival.

For years I solved the business side for myself, one project at a time: my own contracts, my own invoices, my own way of walking a client through a concept. Then the work grew past my own practice. I started placing other artists' work, and I saw the same gaps from the other side of the table. Businesses wanted something real on their walls and had no clear way to get it. Artists wanted to be paid fairly and seen fully, and had no one holding the deal for them.

My art has always been about being seen, and about connecting with the one person in the room who truly takes it in. SOV carries that into business. The brand gets seen for what it stands for, and the artist gets seen for more than a color scheme, a composition or a price.

SOV is what my own workaround became once it had to hold more than one person. Before we built it, we commissioned 315 pages of research citing 2,668 sources, so every question in our brief and every rule in our agreements traces back to something real. Then we built the parts I wish I had when I was starting: a guided brief, clear agreements, approvals and payments in one place, and a record of every finished work.

We started in Greensboro and we are piloting in North Carolina, because this is where I have done the work. The platform is built to work in any city or country.

What SOV is today

SOV is an ArtTech platform and talent marketplace. It has three parts that work together.

The platform
Briefs, agreements, approvals, milestone payments and the archive of every finished work, in one place for the organization and the creative.
The SOV Registry
Independent artists and designers who have applied, been vetted and are reviewed every year. Registry members are matched to commercial work.
Studio
Business infrastructure for every independent artist and designer, including brand architecture templates, a wellness hub and a community, so growing a practice doesn't cost your rights or your mental health.

SOV is short for sovereignty.

The name is the promise. Organizations keep a clear scope, budget and final say. Artists and designers keep their pay, their rights and their name. We hold the deal in between, and we believe the work gets better when people also gather in the same room.
