Our story

Everyone who shapes culture deserves to be paid, protected and seen.

The issue

Creative work drives how places feel and how missions are remembered. The system around it still leaves the people who make it behind.

80¢Earned by women artists for every dollar earned by men.
69%Of BIPOC artists faced unemployment in 2020, against 60% of white artists.
$2.23BA year from North Carolina's nonprofit arts sector alone.

Sources: National Endowment for the Arts, Artists in the Workforce (2015–2019 data). Americans for the Arts, COVID-19 Impact on the Arts (2021). Arts and Economic Prosperity 6, North Carolina Arts Council.

Where it breaks

Organizations become project managers.

Briefs, contracts, insurance and payments land on teams with no time for them.

Creatives work for free first.

Unpaid proposals, handshake deals and late invoices decide who can afford to stay.

The record disappears.

Rights, files and credit scatter across inboxes, and the work loses its history.

Our answer is the system in between.

SOV carries the scope, agreements, approvals, payments and records that let culturally grounded work happen again and again.

Where it began

SOV started in Greensboro, North Carolina, with an artist who spent fourteen years on both sides of the deal: the creative chasing an invoice, and the client unsure who to trust.

Before building, we studied 315 pages of research from 2,668 sources. Every question we ask and every rule we keep traces back to it.
