How it works

Two sides, one deal both can trust.

Hiring a creative shouldn't feel like a gamble. Getting hired shouldn't be unpaid work. Here's what each side does, and everything SOV takes off both plates.

  2. Meet your matches3 to 5 vetted creatives and why each one fits. Pick who gets a paid concept round.

Questions businesses ask

How much should we budget?

Every project is priced on its scope and complexity, and you see the total and every line before you sign. If you have an idea but no brief, a project definition is a fixed fee you can credit toward the project. As a benchmark, many developers set aside about 0.5 to 1 percent of construction cost for art. Tell us your range and we'll show you what it can do.

How long does it take?

A shortlist usually comes within two weeks of a full brief. Make and install depend on the work, and we plan around your opening or renovation date.

What if we don't like the concept?

You give feedback in plain words, and the creative revises. If it still isn't right, you can stop after the paid concept round.

Do we have to sign a long contract?

No. Start with one space. Grow only if it works.

We're a city or public agency. Can SOV run our call for artists?

Yes. We can write the call, share it with our network, collect applications, support your selection panel and handle contracts, while following your procurement rules. If you can hire directly, the regular brief works too.

Do you work outside North Carolina?

We're piloting projects in North Carolina first. The platform is built for any city or country, so tell us where you are and we'll let you know what's possible today.

Our building is historic, or we need permits. Is that a problem?

No, it's part of the job. Tell us in the brief what you know, and we'll check historic district rules, preservation or landmark review, building and fire code, accessibility and local permits before anything is signed, then build the review time into the schedule.

What if something goes wrong?

Every project has a named SOV project lead. Scope changes go through a written change request, so nothing is added or dropped quietly, and the project lead steps in on schedule, quality or payment questions.

Who owns the work?

You own the physical work you buy. The creative keeps the copyright, and any uses you need, like photos for marketing, are written into the deal.
