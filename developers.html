Who we work with

Development principals, project executives and managers, architects, interior designers, construction managers and asset managers.

What's on your plate

Creative work has to fit a construction schedule.

Artists are not usually set up to work inside a GC's process. That gap is where projects slip.

Late design changes

A concept that shifts after CDs ripples into structure, electrical and the schedule.

Fabrication risk

Large work that can't be built as drawn, or can't be built in time.

Install conflicts

Lifts, power and access that collide with trades and the GC's site plan.

Unclear ownership

Who owns the piece, who owns the rights, and what happens when the building changes.

Uninsured vendors

A creative on site without the COI your GC and lender require.

A public-art requirement

A condition of approval that has to be met, documented and signed off.

“SOV translates creative work into the schedules, scopes, approvals and documentation required by a commercial development.”

Questions we hear

Before you reach out.

More answers on how it works.

We have a public-art requirement in our approvals.

We can plan it, run the selection, deliver it and document it for your sign-off, inside your schedule.

Can your creatives work with our GC?

Yes. We coordinate site access, insurance, safety orientation and sequencing with your GC, so the creative works like any other trade.

What happens if we renovate the building later?

Murals can carry artist rights under the federal Visual Artists Rights Act. We address future removal or changes in the agreement up front, so there are no surprises.

Rather talk it through first?

Not ready for a brief? Start with a conversation. A person on our team writes back, and there is no obligation.
